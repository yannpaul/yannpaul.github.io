Operator Precedence

When a line has several operators, which one does Python do first? Learn to read a line the way Python does, and a += 4 + 5 stops being a trick question.

⏱ about 20 min ▶ code you can run on this page 3 practice problems here, 17 in the bank

Your teacher's list ranks the kinds of operators from "happens first" to "happens last". This lesson turns that list into a three-step habit you can apply to any line, and then gives you plenty of lines to practise on.

The ladder from your notes

▲ Happens first

  1. Arithmetic + - * / %
  2. Containment in, not in
  3. Comparison < > == !=
  4. Identity is, is not
  5. Logical not, and, or
  6. Assignment = += -=

▼ Happens last

Two small corrections to your notes

1. Arithmetic has its own little ladder. *, /, // and % happen before + and -, and ** (power) happens before all of those. That is why 2 + 3 * 4 is 14, not 20.

2. In real Python, containment, comparison and identity are one level, not three. in, is, <, == and friends are tied and are worked out left to right. Your class list is fine for nearly every test question, because it is rare for two of them to meet in one line. Lesson 2 shows the one case where it matters. Source: the operator precedence table in the Python docs.

The three-step habit

  1. Circle the operators on the line.
  2. Rank them with the ladder. Parentheses beat everything.
  3. Do the highest first. If two are tied, go left to right. Assignment (=, +=) is always last.

What += really means

a += b is shorthand for a = a + b. The same goes for -=, *=, /=, //= and %=. Everything on the right of the += is worked out first, because assignment is last.
