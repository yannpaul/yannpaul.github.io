The Internet Networking Stack

How a request actually gets from a browser to your code and back — Cloudflare, Kong, Kubernetes, and service-mesh auth — from the operator's side of a SaaS company.

Thirteen short lessons, each a self-contained page with a quick retrieval-practice check. Start at Lesson 1 and work through in order — each one zooms into one hop of the map the first lesson draws, or one concern (auth, error codes, caching) that cuts across all of them. The course is complete, start to capstone.

Lessons

  1. The Map: One Request, Six Hops — the whole path at a glance: DNS → Cloudflare edge → origin load balancer → Kong → Kubernetes Service → Pod, and why it's split into pieces at all
  2. IP Addresses and the VPC — public vs. private address space, what a VPC and its subnets are, and why only the load balancer (not Kong, not your Pods) has an internet-reachable address
  3. CIDR, Route Tables, and Traffic Direction — reading a /16 vs. a /24, how a route table picks a target via longest prefix match, and why ingress being allowed doesn't always mean egress is too
  4. Cloudflare at the Edge — what a PoP does to a request in order (DDoS/rate checks, the WAF, cache lookup), what a cache HIT skips entirely, and the 522/524 errors specific to this one hop
  5. TLS and Certificates — the handshake in one pass, the certificate chain, why Cloudflare's Flexible/Full/Full (Strict) modes decide whether the edge-to-origin leg is encrypted at all, and an mTLS preview
  6. API Gateways and Kong — what a gateway centralizes that a load balancer doesn't, Kong's Services/Routes/Plugins/Consumers model, where end-user authentication actually gets checked, and Kong's own mTLS-secured control-plane/data-plane split
  7. Kubernetes Services and kube-proxy — why Pods need a stable address in front of them, ClusterIP/NodePort/LoadBalancer, EndpointSlices, and what kube-proxy actually does (hint: it's not a proxy in the traffic path)
  8. Pod Networking and the CNI — the flat, NAT-free Pod networking model, veth pairs and the bridge for same-node delivery, overlay vs. underlay/BGP for cross-node, and who actually enforces a NetworkPolicy
  9. Ingress and Kong as Ingress Controller — why Services can't do HTTP-aware routing, what an Ingress resource is (and why it does nothing alone), IngressClass, and the precise sense in which Kong is your Ingress Controller
  10. The Service Mesh and Citadel — sidecar proxies, istiod (where Citadel actually lives now), SPIFFE workload identities, and PeerAuthentication vs. AuthorizationPolicy
  11. AuthN vs. AuthZ, End to End — every layer's authentication/authorization answer lined up in one table, and why static assets skip nearly all of it
  12. Error Codes Across the Stack — a full diagnostic map from a status code to the hop that produced it, including same-code-different-layer traps like a 404 from Kong vs. a 404 from your app
  13. Capstone: Trace a Real Request — interleaved recall across all twelve lessons, two full traces side by side, then a new video-conferencing scenario to test whether it transfers
